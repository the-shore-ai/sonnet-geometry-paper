# Adversarial literature review

Search date: **2026-10-17**, America/Los_Angeles. This is a scoped, reproducible search record, not a systematic-review claim. Primary sources were preferred: author repositories, arXiv full texts, ACL/ICLR/PMLR proceedings, publisher papers, and official model documentation. A verified URL establishes availability of a source, not replication of its results. Bibliographic records are in `paper/references.bib`.

## Finding that changes the framing

**Do not claim that transformer geometry, sonnet trajectories, all-layer authorship analysis, or cross-model similarity are new.** Particularly close public work already examines trajectories across Shakespeare's 154 sonnets. The defensible research question is whether a rigorously controlled, archived, multi-family causal-model experiment finds reproducible structure and incremental predictive value. Even that contribution must be reassessed against an updated search before release.

## Code audit of the closest repository

The public GitHub tree was independently resolved to commit **`4fd12bbbadd58af6d8cc16658bea67b1180b7f4f`**, and specific files were read at that revision. This was static inspection, not a reproduction of saved outputs.

The [sonnet notebook](https://github.com/JustinStec/poetry-bert-formalism/blob/4fd12bbbadd58af6d8cc16658bea67b1180b7f4f/notebooks/shakespeare_sonnets_bert_analysis.ipynb) computes one last-layer BERT mean embedding **per isolated line**, excluding CLS/SEP (cell 8). Its sonnet loop (cell 12) does not preserve preceding lines as context. Its trajectory therefore runs across line embeddings, not transformer layers. Cell 10 sums turning angles and divides by the distance between the first and last line embeddings. The reported couplet metric is simply the distance between the final two line vectors. It compares EEBO-BERT and Poetry-BERT specializations. These observations narrow the overlap without erasing it.

The [analysis module](https://github.com/JustinStec/poetry-bert-formalism/blob/4fd12bbbadd58af6d8cc16658bea67b1180b7f4f/src/poetry_bert/analysis.py) also contains MiniLM sentence-embedding comparisons. The [dataset](https://github.com/JustinStec/poetry-bert-formalism/blob/4fd12bbbadd58af6d8cc16658bea67b1180b7f4f/src/poetry_bert/training/dataset.py) and [losses](https://github.com/JustinStec/poetry-bert-formalism/blob/4fd12bbbadd58af6d8cc16658bea67b1180b7f4f/src/poetry_bert/models/losses.py) implement line/quatrain pairing and hierarchical objectives. Thus hierarchy is supported by code, not only prose; this does not establish that all claimed trained checkpoints or numerical findings are reproducible. Its abstraction “three layers” primarily refers to historical/poetic/prosodic stages.

## Shakespearean stylometry and adversarial authorship evidence

Ahmed Shamsul Arefin, Renato Vimieiro, Carlos Riveros, Hugh Craig and Pablo Moscato (2014), [**An Information Theoretic Clustering Approach for Unveiling Authorship Affinities in Shakespearean Era Plays and Poems**](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0111445), analyzes word-frequency relations among 256 works. The paper and supplementary frequency/distance matrices are available; the underlying corpus includes material assembled from licensed sources. A public derived matrix is not permission to redistribute its source texts. This is close prior art for affinity clustering, and motivates a strong word-frequency/Jensen–Shannon baseline.

Santiago Segarra, Mark Eisen, Gabriel Egan and Alejandro Ribeiro (2016), [**Attributing the Authorship of the Henry VI Plays by Word Adjacency**](https://academic.oup.com/sq/article-abstract/67/2/232/5059164), is a concrete Shakespeare/Marlowe attribution application. Mark Eisen, Santiago Segarra, Gabriel Egan and Alejandro Ribeiro's [**Stylometric Analysis of Early Modern Period English Plays**](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05670), submitted October 18, 2016, develops the broader WAN analysis. Primary papers verified; an immutable, runnable release was not verified in this pass. Their subject is dramatic texts and collaboration, not proof of the Marlovian theory or authorship of the sonnets.

The literature is contested. Pervez Rizvi's [**An analysis of the Word Adjacency Network method—Part 1—The evidence of its unsoundness**](https://academic.oup.com/dsh/article/38/1/347/6587155) appeared online in 2022 and in the 2023 volume. Gabriel Egan, Mark Eisen, Alejandro Ribeiro and Santiago Segarra [responded in 2023](https://www.gabrielegan.com/publications/Eisen%2BSegarra%2BRibeiro%2BEgan2023.pdf). Both primary texts are available. Their dispute makes corpus balance, missing features, threshold calibration and held-out attribution essential. We do not adjudicate it by selecting the conclusion favorable to our hypothesis.

Even neural-network Shakespeare/Marlowe attribution is old: Matt Tearle, Kye Taylor and Howard Demuth's [**An algorithm for automated authorship attribution using neural networks**](https://academic.oup.com/dsh/article-abstract/23/4/425/1037856) was published in 2008. Transformer activations may improve a measured task, but replacing features does not by itself make the authorship question new.

## Search log and limitations

Representative queries used: `"Shakespeare" "sonnets" "Qwen"`; `"Shakespeare" "sonnets" "Gemma"`; `"Shakespeare" "activation geometry"`; `"Shakespeare" "sonnets" transformer representations`; `"authorship attribution" "hidden states" transformer`; `"PoPE" positional embeddings`; `"Shakespeare" "Marlowe" "word adjacency networks"`; `"Mechanistic interpretability" "poetry" transformer`; and exact title searches for the papers above. Searches included author-hosted artifacts as well as proceedings.

No fully matching published experiment combining all 154 sonnets, full token-level residual streams, three current model families, and controlled cross-family agreement was verified in this pass. **This is a search outcome, not evidence of absence or a novelty claim.** Qwen poetry interpretability and sonnet trajectory work did appear. Indexing gaps, unpublished notebooks, renamed repositories, and inaccessible material remain possible. A browser-check page blocked one OpenReview PDF; its PoPE sonnet analysis was subsequently checked in arXiv HTML. Dates are taken from primary pages, not search-engine relative-age labels.

Before public release: archive cited code revisions, inspect and reproduce the closest BERT tortuosity computation, verify corpus edition overlap, inspect the primary manuscripts beyond abstract-level claims where needed, search citations forward/backward, and obtain a literary scholar's review. Keep unsuccessful replication and negative findings in the record.


## Follow-up search addendum — 2026-10-18

This addendum preserves the October 17 search record above and records closer sources found during the subsequent analysis. Their late discovery strengthens the caution against priority claims; it does not make the earlier scoped search exhaustive.

**Haram Lee (2025), [“Sentiment analysis of the volta in Shakespeare’s sonnets”](https://academic.oup.com/dsh/article/40/2/562/8078794), *Digital Scholarship in the Humanities* 40(2):562–574, DOI 10.1093/llc/fqaf018.** Publisher metadata, the full method, and the associated [Zenodo dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/14965925) were inspected. This is direct precedent for computational study of the sonnets’ rhetorical turns. Its qualitative form scheme assumes a turn at line 13 and distinguishes optional line-nine turns through markers or thematic interpretation. It is therefore inappropriate as independent evidence of a universal line-13 turn. The separate sentiment exercise used collective adjudication; independent multi-annotator reliability for form labels was not established. Dataset metadata explicitly licenses the release CC BY 4.0. Unchanged CSVs, attribution and hashes are preserved in [the external-source directory](../datasets/external/lee2025/README.md). Bibliography keys: `lee2025`, `lee2025data`.

**Nicolò Busetto and Rodolfo Delmonte (2019), [“Annotating Shakespeare’s Sonnets with Appraisal Theory to Detect Irony”](https://aclanthology.org/2019.clicit-1.11/), CLiC-it 2019, pp.55–62.** The proceedings record and paper were inspected. It provides direct precedent for annotated rhetorical/appraisal analysis of this corpus. The authors describe checking and correcting annotations but do not report formal independent interannotator agreement; the stated correction rate is not a substitute for that measurement. A complete public machine-readable annotation release and its license were not verified. Bibliography key: `busetto2019`.

These sources narrow the contribution to controlled evaluation of representation choices, rather than first discovery of sonnet rhetoric. The subsequent study froze Lee’s qualitative shadow-versus-novel line-nine labels before testing model association, checked relevant edition differences, and retained the negative added-value result. Protocols, source limitations and falsifiable follow-ups are recorded in [the research-opportunities review](deep-study-opportunities.md); the original literature-review table remains a dated record rather than an assertion about the current measured results.
